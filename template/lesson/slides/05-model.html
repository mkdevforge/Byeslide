Move the Moon around Earth

Drag the Moon or the slider. The white curve marks the half of the Moon that faces Earth.

About 8 minutes. This is the heart of the lesson. The left side is a view from far above the North Pole, with the Sun far off to the left. The right side is what we see from Earth, from the Northern Hemisphere. In the Southern Hemisphere the picture is flipped left to right. Start by clicking the new moon button under the slider: the Moon is between Earth and the Sun, and we see only its dark night side. Ask: "Which half of the Moon is lit?" Always the half facing the Sun. "Which half can we see?" The half facing Earth, marked by the white curve. The phase is the part of the lit half that we can see. Drag the Moon slowly around, or press Play a month. Stop at first quarter (lit on the right), full moon (Earth is between the Sun and the Moon, and we see the whole lit half) and third quarter (lit on the left). Point out the day counter: one full cycle takes 29.5 days. The model keeps the Sun still and is not to scale: the real Moon is about 30 Earth widths away.
