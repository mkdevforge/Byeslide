Three facts to start with

The Moon makes no light of its own.

Moonlight is sunlight that bounces off the Moon.

The Sun always lights half of the Moon.

The half that faces the Sun has day. The other half has night.

The Moon goes around Earth about once a month.

From one new moon to the next takes 29.5 days.

About 3 minutes. Click once for each fact. Fact 1: many pupils think the Moon shines like a lamp. Ask: "If the Sun went out, could we still see the Moon?" No, because moonlight is sunlight that bounces off the Moon. Fact 2: hold up a ball next to a window or lamp and show that one half is always lit. Fact 3: the Moon goes around Earth in about 27.3 days if you measure against the stars, but the phases take 29.5 days to repeat, because Earth moves along its path around the Sun at the same time. Pupils only need "about a month". In every drawing in this lesson the Sun is on the left, and we look down on Earth from above the North Pole.
