Lunar eclipse

  • Earth's shadow falls on the Moon. It turns a dim red.
  • Only at full moon, and not at every full moon.

About 4 minutes. Go back to the reasons pupils gave for A on slide 2. Explain: Earth's shadow points away from the Sun, so it can only reach the Moon when the Sun, Earth and the Moon are in a line, with Earth in the middle. That is full moon. When it happens, it is a lunar eclipse. It lasts a few hours, and the Moon often turns a dim red, because Earth's air bends a little red sunlight into the shadow. Most full moons have no eclipse, because the Moon's path is tilted by about 5 degrees, so it usually passes just above or below the shadow. Phases happen every night.
