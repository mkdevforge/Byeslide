# Is it Earth's shadow?

**Phases**

Drawing, top view: the Moon is beside Earth, outside Earth's shadow, and half of it is lit. Labels: Moon, Earth's shadow.

- We see them all month long.
- The dark part is the Moon's own night side.

**Lunar eclipse**

Drawing, top view: the Sun, Earth and the Moon in a line, with the Moon inside Earth's shadow. Labels: Moon, Earth's shadow.

- Earth's shadow falls on the Moon. It turns a dim red.
- Only at full moon, and not at every full moon.

## Speaker notes

About 4 minutes. Go back to the reasons pupils gave for A on slide 2. Explain: Earth's shadow points away from the Sun, so it can only reach the Moon when the Sun, Earth and the Moon are in a line, with Earth in the middle. That is full moon. When it happens, it is a lunar eclipse. It lasts a few hours, and the Moon often turns a dim red, because Earth's air bends a little red sunlight into the shadow. Most full moons have no eclipse, because the Moon's path is tilted by about 5 degrees, so it usually passes just above or below the shadow. Phases happen every night. Then click to show the answer to the vote. Extension: a solar eclipse is the opposite. The Moon's shadow falls on Earth, and that can only happen at new moon.
