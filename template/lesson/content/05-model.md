# Move the Moon around Earth

Drag the Moon or the slider. The white curve marks the half of the Moon that faces Earth.

The model has two views of the same moment.

**Top view.** Sunlight comes from one side, Earth is in the middle, and the Moon goes around Earth. The half of the Moon that faces the Sun is lit. Labels: Sunlight, Earth, Moon. Caption: Seen from above the North Pole. Not to scale.

**The Moon as seen from Earth**, with the phase name, the day and how much of the Moon is lit, for example: **Waxing crescent**, Day 4 of 29.5, 15% lit. Caption: Seen from Earth's Northern Hemisphere.

The sentence under the Moon, for each phase:

- New moon: We see only the Moon's night side.
- Waxing crescent: Lit on the right, and growing.
- First quarter: Half of the side we see is lit.
- Waxing gibbous: More than half lit, and growing.
- Full moon: We see the whole sunlit half.
- Waning gibbous: More than half lit, and shrinking.
- Third quarter: Half lit again, on the left this time.
- Waning crescent: Lit on the left, and shrinking.

Controls:

- **Play a month**: the Moon goes once around Earth in 14 seconds. Press again to pause.
- A slider for the days since new moon, from 0 to 29. It starts at day 4.
- One button for each of the eight phases, in order. Each one moves the Moon to that phase.

## Speaker notes

About 8 minutes. This is the heart of the lesson. The left side is a view from far above the North Pole, with the Sun far off to the left. The right side is what we see from Earth, from the Northern Hemisphere. In the Southern Hemisphere the picture is flipped left to right. Start by clicking the new moon button under the slider: the Moon is between Earth and the Sun, and we see only its dark night side. Ask: "Which half of the Moon is lit?" Always the half facing the Sun. "Which half can we see?" The half facing Earth, marked by the white curve. The phase is the part of the lit half that we can see. Drag the Moon slowly around, or press Play a month. Stop at first quarter (lit on the right), full moon (Earth is between the Sun and the Moon, and we see the whole lit half) and third quarter (lit on the left). Point out the day counter: one full cycle takes 29.5 days. The model keeps the Sun still and is not to scale: the real Moon is about 30 Earth widths away. Return to the vote: which explanation fits what the model shows?
