# What we found out

- The Sun always lights half of the Moon.
- As the Moon goes around Earth, we see more or less of that lit half.
- A full cycle of phases takes 29.5 days.

**Homework: keep a Moon diary**

Each evening for a week, look for the Moon. Draw what you see, and write the date and time.

No Moon? Write "not seen" or "cloudy". That is an observation too.

The Moon diary: seven boxes, Night 1, Night 2, Night 3, Night 4, Night 5, Night 6 and Night 7. Each has a circle to draw the Moon in and lines for the Date and the Time. This slide prints as the homework sheet.

## Speaker notes

About 2 minutes. Read the three points together; ask pupils to finish each sentence before you read it. Print this slide as the homework sheet: run pnpm pdf and print this page, one per pupil. Set the homework in the week after a new moon, when the Moon is in the evening sky: a waxing crescent first, then first quarter, then waxing gibbous. Check a Moon calendar for the dates. In the waning half of the month the Moon rises late at night, so pupils would have to look in the morning instead. Next lesson, put the diaries side by side and order them by date: the class will have drawn the phases themselves.
