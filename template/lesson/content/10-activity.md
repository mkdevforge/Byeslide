# Be the Earth: make the phases yourself

**You need**

- A lamp without a shade: the Sun
- A pale ball on a pencil: the Moon
- Your head: Earth
- A dark room

**Safety:** do not stare into the lamp. It gets hot.

The steps, one per click:

1. Face the lamp. Hold the ball at arm's length, a little higher than your head.
2. You see the dark side of the ball. This is new moon.
3. Turn slowly to your left. Keep the ball in front of your face. Watch the lit part grow on the right.
4. Stop with your back to the lamp. Keep the ball out of your head's shadow. This is full moon.
5. Keep turning left until you face the lamp again. Name each phase as you go.

## Speaker notes

About 7 minutes. Pupils work alone or in pairs around one lamp; up to about eight pupils can stand in a circle around the same lamp. Put the lamp at head height in the middle of the room and make the room as dark as you can. Click to show one step at a time and do each step together. In step 3, turning left is the same direction the Moon goes around Earth, seen from above the North Pole. At first quarter the lamp is on your right and the ball is lit on the right, just like the model. In step 4, if your head's shadow falls on the ball, you have made a lunar eclipse: hold the ball a little higher, the way the Moon's tilted path usually passes above or below Earth's shadow. Ask pupils to stop at first quarter and third quarter and say which side is lit. Polystyrene balls on pencils work well. A phone torch can stand in for the lamp in a small group.
